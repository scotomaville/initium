summary: >-
  There is a hole you know by feel—the same misstep, again. Bring to mind a pattern you recognize
  mainly after you’ve fallen into it. Nelson’s Autobiography (Portia Nelson’s street) asks what
  changes when you see the hole earlier, and whether another street is actually available this time.
  Returning awareness can open a different walk; it doesn’t erase the falls already taken. Choose a
  slope to stay with that recognition—not to shame the fall.
sherpa_version: Nelson's Poem's like spotting the same pothole you keep hitting - once you see it, you can start walking around
  it.
reasoning: This principle finds blind-spots in repeating pitfalls, reframing them as growth signals. A Providential nudge
  from the Prodigal Son sparks awareness, turning falls into choices. It escalates from noticing patterns to comprehending
  change, enabling action with Frankl’s attitude and Philippians’ strength.
expert_high: Portia Nelson
expert_high_quote: I walk down another street.
expert_high_reason: Nelson’s poem charts falling into the same hole repeatedly until choosing a new street, reframing pitfalls
  as progressive steps toward self-discovery. As a therapist and author, her work stemmed from personal journeys of awareness
  and change. Links Frankl’s choice to Prodigal Son. Supports Maslow’s growth-to-transcendence and Bloom’s creating paths,
  nudging conscious transformation.
expert_medium: Viktor Frankl

expert_medium_reason: Frankl’s Holocaust survival emphasized choosing attitude amid despair, reframing life’s pitfalls as
  opportunities for meaning and agency. Drawing from his logotherapy developed through suffering, he showed resilience in
  extreme adversity. Links Prodigal Son to Philippians’ strength. Supports Maslow’s esteem-to-growth shift and Bloom’s analyzing
  experiences, nudging purposeful action.

  in human struggles. Links to Frankl’s choice. Supports Maslow’s esteem-to-growth shift and Bloom’s analyzing patterns, nudging
  aware change.
