summary: >-
  Camp 2 asks for Knowing WHY while habits may still be running on autopilot. Think of something you
  keep doing—work, speech, avoidance, pursuit—whose real motive you have not named lately. WIDWID
  digs for the core reason and emotional trigger beneath the pattern so intention can catch up with
  motion. Motives show in repeated action more clearly than in explanations you give yourself. Name
  the why you are actually living.
sherpa_version: WIDWID (Why I Do What I Do)'s asking 'why' behind actions - like figuring out why you eat junk food before
  bed.
reasoning: This strategy digs up blind-spots in routine, reframing motives as purpose. A Providential nudge from the Brother
  and Sister sparks intent, turning habits into meaning. It escalates from noticing actions to grasping why, enabling action
  with  Gardner's pursuit of passion and Matthew’s fruits.
expert_high: Viktor Frankl
expert_high_quote: Those who have a ‘why' to live can bear almost any ‘how'.
expert_high_reason: Frankl’s why for living bears any how, reframing struggles as meaningful. In Holocaust logotherapy, he
  found purpose. Links Socrates’ probe to Brother and Sister. Supports Maslow’s growth-to-transcendence and Bloom’s evaluating
  meaning, nudging providential drive.
expert_medium: Chris Gardner
expert_medium_quote: 'The secret to success: find something you love to do so much, you can''t wait for the sun to rise to
  do it all over again.'
expert_medium_reason: Gardner's pursuit of passion reframes daily actions as purposeful drive, born from adversity. In his
  Pursuit of Happyness story, he overcame homelessness through self-belief. Links Aesop's fruit-discernment to Frankl's why-for-living.
  Supports Maslow’s self-actualization and Bloom’s creating meaning, nudging competent application of insights.

  purpose.
