summary: >-
  The crowd already has an answer, and it’s easy to wear it. Bring to mind a choice where popular
  opinion and what seems true are pulling apart. Popular Vs True asks which one you’re actually
  following—the broad consensus, or the narrower reading of what is so. Being interrupted toward
  what’s real can cost belonging; that cost is part of the terrain. Choose a slope to test the
  difference—not to win an argument with the crowd.
sherpa_version: Popular Vs True's picking real over trendy - like choosing what works, not what's hot.
reasoning: This principle digs up blind-spots in equating popularity with truth, reframing trends as distractions. A Providential
  nudge from Andersen’s child calls for discernment, aligning with reality. It escalates from spotting bias to comprehending
  integrity, fostering action with Bonhoeffer’s interruption and Matthew’s gate.
expert_high: Matthew
expert_high_quote: Matthew's narrow gate (7:13-14)
expert_high_reason: Matthew’s narrow gate parable urges choosing truth’s rugged path over popularity’s broad way, reframing
  trends as fleeting distractions from eternal reality. Drawing from Jesus’ teachings on discernment, Matthew’s gospel reflects
  his transformation from tax collector to apostle. Links Bonhoeffer’s resistance to Andersen’s honesty. Supports Maslow’s
  growth-to-transcendence and Bloom’s evaluating paths, nudging divine clarity.
expert_medium: Dietrich Bonhoeffer
expert_medium_quote: We must be ready to allow ourselves to be interrupted by God. (Life Together, 1939)
expert_medium_reason: Bonhoeffer’s courageous resistance to Nazi ideology chooses divine truth over popular conformity, reframing
  societal pressure as a call to interruption and integrity. As a theologian facing persecution and death, his life embodied
  costly discipleship. Links Andersen’s honesty to Matthew’s gate. Supports Maslow’s belonging-to-growth shift and Bloom’s
  analyzing choices, nudging courageous truth-seeking.

  trends, nudging honest discernment over crowd pressure.
