## Left Page (verso — card content)

REFINED EXPLANATION:
DEFINITION:
Biblical & Stoic Wisdom is a harmonized framework that draws from Biblical teachings and Stoic philosophy to guide
Explorers in building resilience, practicing virtue, and making strategic decisions aligned with higher purpose.
Clarity: This principle acts as a compass following ancient truths, offering direction for a life of integrity and strength. Biblical wisdom provides divine insight - like a lantern illuminating the path - while
Stoicism supplies practical discipline, like a steady hand on a fixed rope.
360MEN: Learning From Failure:
“By refining our questions we can glean wisdom from others - all across history. Equally, we can find wisdom by listening to our inner ‘knower’. Example: Discretion is the better part of valor. It’s knowing when to step out in faith, when to stand firm and when to step aside.”
Actionability: 
Apply this by selecting a Biblical verse or Stoic maxim, reflecting on how it can be added to their next prompt to inform their next decision. It’s a simple yet profound way to root choices in truth.
DANIEL’S CAIRN:
As a new believer, I typically ate lunch on my construction site, sitting on the floor of my VW bus - cargo door open - as the Denver sun melted the glistening snow. The FM radio handing me insights to chew on from Chuck Swindoll, Chuck
Smith, and James Dobson. I imagined a day when 
I might have wisdom to share, with the same depth of experience as those mentors. What I failed to see was the internet, and emergence of AI, and yet the baton passed to me by those men is still in my hand. It’s a mouse. It’s a smartphone. It’s a story of numerous expeditions and thousands of days of anguish, doubt, and storms. Fortunately, I practiced. I met sages along the way. What I mean to hand you now is a glimpse of YOUR journey. Celebrate small climbs each day. Record gratitude, and feed your AI with wisdom, in the way it should go.
Sherpa Grok PROMPT:
The three experts—Epictetus, C.S. Lewis, and the 
Apostle Paul—collectively emphasize achieving inner peace and contentment through a blend of rational self-control and faith-based trust.
APPLICATION:
Practical Step: 
Pick a Biblical verse (e.g., Proverbs 3:5-6, “Trust in the Lord with all your heart”) or a Stoic quote (e.g.,
Marcus Aurelius, “You have power over your mind 
- not outside events”) and prompt how it applies to 
a current challenge - like managing stress.
Epictetus wrote: “It’s not things that upset us, but our judgments about them.” He taught us how to master resilience by mastering inner control.
PAIRS WITH:
Strategies: Pairs with Self-Reflection 
(to internalize wisdom) and Providential Alignment (to act in harmony with higher guidance).
C.S. Lewis (Mere Christianity), concluded that we should “aim at Heaven and you will get earth thrown in.” because he overcame great loss through personal losses. :
Tools: Supported by Checkpoint (to assess alignment with virtues) and
Gratitude Reflection (to appreciate growth).
Mindset Shift: 
Move from “What feels good now?” to “What aligns with virtue and purpose?” Think of it like a climber choosing the harder, safer route over the quick descent—prioritizing integrity over ease.
POTENTIAL CHALLENGE:
Wildcards: Links to Providential 
Guidance, reinforcing trust in a greater plan.
Misconception: Some might see this principle as rigid or outdated, a relic of the past.
Paul’s letter (Hebrews 13:5 ) encouraged persecuted believers to trust God’s provision. “Keep your lives free from the love of money and be content with what you have, because God has said, ‘Never will I leave you; never will I forsake you.’”

Like a climber combining maps and instincts, the super-union of Biblical & Stoic wisdom reframes storms as provident tests and applies to modern AI training, where it encourages ethical self-mastery over unchecked ambition.
Mitigation: Highlight its timeless utility—like a climber adapting ancient techniques to modern peaks. It’s not dogma but a flexible toolkit for resilience, proven across centuries.
My jobsite has changed but my mission has not. Listen to those that have fallen and yet still climb.
“Follow in-the-way they began.”
