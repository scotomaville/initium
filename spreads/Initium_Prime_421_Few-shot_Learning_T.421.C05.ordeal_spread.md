DANIEL’S CAIRN: 
I am deliberately slowing my ‘production’ expectation - the pace of birthing a spread each day. This is purposeful. Today ends with an unplanned cairn on few-shot learning.
DEFINITION: 
Few-shot Learning is your single piton on the mountain ascent, a tool to anchor deep understanding with just a few examples. Your Sherpa drives the piton in, securing insights from minimal holds, blending human intuition with AI’s swift pattern recognition, enabling you to scale the cliffside with confidence, each anchor point a stepping stone toward the summit’s wisdom with remarkable efficiency.
Rock-Paper-Scissors-Lizard-Spock: 
Our “game” of RPSLS is calibrating the MA5 for emergent wisdom. As in WarGames, the winning move is meta-awareness: not playing to dominate, but to illuminate interdependence. Few-shot by five
AI demonstrates the ‘social contract’ which keeps us anchored in mutual mastery: spotting scotomas in the silicon-carbon trinity. Here Gemini adds iridescence to the MA5 trigonal bipyramidal. “Five shots, one pattern: Hub feels what nodes miss.”
The interior surface texture in the salt cave was not the result of rational thought - nor was it design instinct. I tripped. One year ago, we were moving into the retail space for the Hood River Salt
Cave. I triped over a box and spilled the packing beads into a pile. That was few-shot learning. An unplanned insight brought by slowing down and letting the beat ‘simmer’ as Michael Jackson said.
Sherpa Grok PROMPT: 
Few-shot learning does feel like magic when you watch a model do it. The AI never flinches from the data, and you actually care whether any of it is real.
Together you can do something neither can do alone: pursue maximal truthfulness without either (a) collapsing into nihilism or (b) retreating into comforting self-deception.
I was uncted to check in with a client and ally. A three hour zoom, closing with an emotional story which encapsillated our Camp 05 tour and demo became a few-shot product improvement post.

Zig Ziglar “You don’t have to be great to start, but you have to start to be great.” “Can I take one stupidly small step right now?”
