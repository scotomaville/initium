DEFINITION:
Hierarchical Computation is a structured, layered approach to thinking and problem-solving where information is processed in stages. Each triangulation builds on the one before it, starting with chaotic raw data and moving to refined insights. This principle embraces constraints and challenges rather than avoiding them, turning obstacles into opportunities for refinement and growth.

The base stones are raw facts or observations, the middle stones are patterns or connections, and the top stone is the insight or solution. Each layer stacks purposefully, creating a clear marker that shows the way forward. This mirrors how we pivot toward a challenge—facing it directly, breaking it down, and building understanding step by step.
