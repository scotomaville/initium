## Right Page (recto — visual content)

REFINED EXPLANATION:
Mitigation: Maslow debunked the “all-or-nothing” progression. He noted that needs coexist and influence each other simultaneously—e.g., someone in survival mode can still spark creativity through a sense of purpose. Moses didn’t “complete” his need for safety before the burning bush called him to transcendence; the overlap was the call.
Clarity: 
Picture Maslow’s Extended Hierarchy as a mountain with six camps, each a waypoint in your ascent to Gnosis. Maslow himself reframed self-actualization not as a distant summit but as an ongoing process intertwined with self-transcendence
- the pinnacle he added just before his death .
DANIEL’S CAIRN: 
When Angelina felt a strong pull to create a healing salt cave - which became a sacred space for reflection and growth - we were living full-time in our Airstream, parked in a tough spot. Money was tight; we faced the real threat of losing everything.
It proves a key point: By letting go of the chase for certainty, comfort or security, we unlocked deeper meaning and showed that you can do the same.
APPLICATION:
Practical Step: 
Moses’s ”lower” needs (safety in Midian) were upended not by fulfillment, but by a divine interruption that demanded transcendence. It’s a normal route, not an exceptional one, because Maslow came to see such calls as inherent to human flourishing, accessible to anyone willing to heed them
- much like the prophets who left “settled” lives for 
the wilderness of mission.
POTENTIAL CHALLENGE:
Misconception: The “Rigid Ladder” Fallacy
This common pitfall is the belief that Maslow’s hierarchy functions like a strict, linear ladder or set of isolated “modules”—where you must climb rung by rung, fully nailing each lower need (e.g., food and shelter) before even glimpsing the next (like love or purpose). It’s often visualized as that iconic pyramid, implying self-contained levels that demand complete satisfaction in sequence, with no bleed-over or shortcuts. This turns the model into a checklist for “unlocking” growth, fostering the idea that unmet basics block all higher pursuits, leading to excuses like “I can’t chase my calling until I’m financially secure.” In reality, as Maslow clarified later, needs are far more fluid and interdependent, not sealed-off silos.
