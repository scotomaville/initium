DEFINITION:
Onion Peeling is a reflective strategy where you repeatedly asks “why” to peel back layers of assumptions, emotions, or actions, revealing the core purpose or truth beneath—like chiseling through rock to uncover the past because:
“The unexamined life is not worth living,”
SV REFLECTION v1.07 Onion Peeling:
“Peeling back layers of our memory helps a great deal to ferret out the root of the misunderstanding and false conclusion we made. The good news is that onion peeling is effective. Don’t swallow what I say. Test it for yourself.”

Onion Peeling uncovers truth by repeatedly asking WHY? to strip away assumptions and distortions. Bob Moawad pointed out the progression: Beliefs >

## Right Page (recto — visual content)

REFINED EXPLANATION:
POTENTIAL CHALLENGE:
Misconception: Onion Peeling leads to overthinking or analysis paralysis.
Clarity: Onion Peeling is like chiseling through layers of rock—each “why” question chips away at surface-level thoughts, revealing the bedrock of your true motivations and beliefs.
Mitigation: 
Limit the process to five “whys” and focus on actionable insights—use Intention Scoring to ensure the uncovered purpose aligns with your goals.
Actionability: 
Use this strategy by asking “why” five times in response to a thought, emotion, or action, each time digging to uncover the root cause or purpose.
DANIEL’S CAIRN
Structured Reasoning: Builds a logical path from surface thoughts to core truths.
Self-Reflection: Encourages deep introspection to understand one’s “why.”
Onion Peeling is a fascinating strategy which is both effective and rewarding. It begins with Affirmations rich with self-trust. What I mean is that the way out of self tyranny is found in a pit inside one’s mind. At first, exploration is frightful. But after discovering that the cause of the ‘pain’ turns out to be nothing more than a tiny stone, you’ll discover the greatest Expedition you’ll ever undertake!
King Solomon was correct:
APPLICATION:
Practical Step: 
Choose a recurring thought, emotion, or action. 
Ask “why” successively, writing down each answer. 
For example: “Why am I procrastinating? Because I’m overwhelmed. Why am I overwhelmed? Because I have too many tasks. Why do I have too many tasks? Because I don’t prioritize well.” Continue to a core insight.

Mindset Shift: 
Admit your blind spots, and inquiry begins. Move from “I know why I do this” to “I’m 100% wrong 50% of the time, so there’s always a deeper reason to uncover.”
“Whoever digs a pit
will fall into it...”
