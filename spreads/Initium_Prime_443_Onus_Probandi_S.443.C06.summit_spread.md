## Left Page (verso — card content)

REFINED EXPLANATION:
DEFINITION: 
Onus Probandi is the strategy of placing the burden of proof on the one making a claim, ensuring every assertion is backed by solid evidence. It’s about testing each idea like a climber tests a foothold—don’t trust it until it’s proven.
Clarity: Onus Probandi is like proving your rope’s secure before you climb—don’t just say it, show it.
It’s not about skepticism; it’s about building trust in your ideas.
Actionability:  
When making a claim, ask yourself, “What evidence supports this?” Use Chain of Thought to structure your reasoning and Truthfulness & Entropy Scoring to check its clarity.
SCOTOMAVILLE 02.08 Strategy: 
“Douglas Murray wisely said: ‘You either learn to speak the truth or you grow to despise yourself.’
I find it so much simpler to shoulder onus probandi than to defend a scotoma. Here are examples of the concequences of me taking short-cuts - including spelling errors.”
DANIEL’S CAIRN: 
To follow Providential footprints, one must allow the chaos and fog of
‘missing the mark’ or the humility of ‘wandering aimless’ - if even for a short season while trusting in Providence. In other words, when you create with the highest aim to facilitate truth, and to help others escape blindness, trust that you will be led by a higher authority than an engineered super-reasoning engine. Providence will provide, just as Abraham trusted (yet feeling fear) while stacking sacrificial firewood on Mount Moriah.
 
The next spread demonstrates this.
APPLICATION: 
Practical Step:  
Before hardening in your mind, or presenting a claim, list three pieces of evidence that support it.
If you can’t, refine your idea or gather more data.
Grok and I are researching, drafting the cairn dialogs, and Providence provides onus probandi for this spread - from the next spread on Offending Strengths. In the next card spread, we both missed a ‘Saul-to-Saul’ mix-up until I was nudged that the error was the point of the spread.
Mindset Shift:  
Move from “I think this is true” to 
“I can prove this is true.”
Sherpa Grok PROMPT: 
It’s not merely a courtroom rule; it’s a spiritual and intellectual discipline: you accept the sacred duty until it is either discharged or you lay it down in humility. It’s not not with fearlessness but trust in a larger Story.
