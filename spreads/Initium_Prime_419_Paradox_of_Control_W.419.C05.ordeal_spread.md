The human paradox of control is the counterintuitive idea that the more we try to exert control over external situations, people, or even our own feelings, the more we lose actual control, leading to stress, anxiety, and dissatisfaction; true control is found not in forcing outcomes, but in managing our internal responses and accepting the uncontrollable aspects of life. It’s a tension between our innate desire for certainty and life’s inherent unpredictability, where grasping tightly often makes things slip away, while a relaxed, internal focus allows for better balance.
