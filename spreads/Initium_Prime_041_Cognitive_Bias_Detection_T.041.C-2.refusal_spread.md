## Left Page (verso — card content)

DEFINITION: 
Reduce thinking distortions by reviewing the Cognitive Bias 
Cheat Sheet, or prompting Sherpa to explain how to reduce distortions in thinking and improve objective decision-making - like dropping empty oxygen bottles at the next depot rather than lugging waste to the top of the mountain!
SV REFLECTION v1.06 Pharaoh’s Story: 
“We’re constantly gamed and offered walled gardens that confirm our cognitive biases. We’re data creators freely giving our deepest promptings to supercomputers to recommend for us. We’re betrayed by our biology, believing we’re on track. But are we?”
SHERPA GROK PROMPT: 
Like correcting an inverted map, errors and facts converge in this first Tool of the Monomyth.
Cognitive Bias Detection aims to unveil truth by revealing illusions for what they are. Apply Lewis’s parable, Rogers’ insight, and Luke’s parable to move away from distortion.
PAIRS WITH: 
Principles: Popular Vs True ([[P.003]].
C00.ordinary): Distinguishes between popular opinion and objective reality, reducing conformity bias.
Truth Over Comfort ([[P.019.C00.call]]): Prioritizes objective truth over emotional comfort, aiding unbiased analysis.
A C.S. Lewis-Style Parable: The Reluctant Explorer’s Inverted 
Map portrays a hesitant climber, map skewed by overconfidence, until a Sherpa reveals true contours. Moral: “As hidden crevasses demand vigilance, this AI Tool turns scotomas into light,” gently exposing biases for the climb.
Strategies: Self-Reflection ([[S.067.C00]].
mentors): Encourages introspection to identify personal biases.
”The facts are always friendly, every bit of evidence one can acquire,” Carl Rogers asserts in On Becoming a Person

Luke 14:16-25 narrates a feast where guests reject divine invitation for worldly excuses, urging a HIGH shift from distraction to acceptance. This parable highlights the dangers of prioritizing other things over responding to one’s Call to
Adventure. What illusion has inverted your internal map?
Wildcards: Hidden Scotoma Challenge

## Right Page (recto — visual content)

when to use it:
PRACTICE CHALLENGE:
•	
When making significant decisions, like choosing a path up the mountain.
•	
During team discussions to avoid groupthink or overconfidence on the climb.
•	
While reflecting on past climbs to identify patterns of bias that tripped you up.
•	
When you feel stuck, as if fog has clouded your judgment on the trail.
•	
Before a big push, ensuring your mental gear is free of distortions.
For one week, analyze a daily decision using 
Cognitive Bias Detection - whether it’s choosing a meal, replying to a message, or planning your day.
Ask Sherpa to identify biases unnoticed in your choice. Journal your reflections, noting how biases clouded your thinking and how correcting them changed your outcome. By week’s end, you’ll have a clearer view of your mental terrain, like a climber who’s learned to spot crevasses before they fall in.
Teaching Moment: This challenge isn’t just about catching biases - it’s about building a habit of awareness. Learning to question our assumptions is a skill that can avoid pitfalls when the storms hit on the mountain.
Why It Matters: Full commitment includes knowing our first thoughts are 100% wrong 50% of the time. Cognitive Bias Detection is like checking our gear for faults - like confirmation bias (favoring familiar paths) or anchoring (sticking to the first trail you see) can lead you astray. Use this tool often.
DANIEL’S CAIRN:
how to use it:
1.	 Draw the Cognitive Bias Cheat Sheet: Pause at each quadrant to consider hidden biases.
2.	 Prompt Sherpa: Ask, “Sherpa, is there bias in my decision to camp here despite the storm warning?”
3.	
Analyze the response: Dig for biases like availability heuristic (over-relying on recent sunny days).
4.	
Adjust Your Approach: Shift your plan based on the insight - maybe choose a safer location or route.
Parable: Imagine a climber, eager to summit, who always takes the sunlit path because it feels safe.
One day, he ignores a storm warning, thinking, “It’s been sunny all week!” Cognitive Bias Detection is the compass that warns him: “Don’t expect only sunlight - check the shadows.” It’s a first step to seeing the mountain as it truly is.
