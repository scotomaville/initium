## Left Page (verso — card content)

DEFINITION:
Nelson’s Autobiography Principle is the transformative power of self-awareness. Our adaptation of Portia Nelson’s poem uses the metaphor of falling into a crevasse to depict the journey from unconscious habit to conscious choice.
Autobiography in Five Short Chapters:
I.   I walk down the trail.
There is a deep crevasse in my path.
I fall in. I am lost. I am helpless.
It isn’t my fault.
It takes forever to find a way out.
II.  I walk down the same trail.
There is a deep crevasse in my path.
I pretend I don’t see it.
I fall in again.
I can’t believe I am in the same place.
It isn’t my fault.
It still takes a long time to get out.
PAIRS WITH:
Strategies: 
Ties to Self-Reflection for deeper understanding and WIDWID (Why I
Do What I Do) to uncover motivations behind recurring habits.
III.  I walk down the same trail.
Tools: Enhanced by Checkpoint (monitoring growth) and Gratitude Reflection (cultivating clarity through past lessons).
There is a deep crevasse in my path.
I see it there, I still fall in... It’s habit. 
My eyes are open. I know where I am.
It is my fault. I get out immediately.
Wildcards: 
Connects to Hidden Scotoma Challenge, urging Explorers to uncover the 
“holes” in their awareness.
IV.  I walk down the same trail.
There is a deep deep crevasse in my path.
I walk around it.
V.  I walk down a different trail.

## Right Page (recto — visual content)

A universal truth
REFINED EXPLANATION:
Clarity: Awareness is about seeing reality as it is—free from the fog of bias or illusion. It’s the clear-eyed view a mountaineer relies on to read the ridgeline ahead - and the pathway up.
Actionability: Explorers cultivate awareness by pausing to survey their inner terrain—akin to a climber assessing the shifting winds or footing before the next step.

APPLICATION:
Practical Step: Reflect on a recurring challenge, as if walking the same trail. Use Nelson’s poem to identify your chapter—are you falling in unaware, or walking around the crevasse?
Portia Nelson’s poem captures a universal truth about the human condition: we often repeat the same mistakes, blind to our own patterns, until self-awareness breaks the cycle. Each chapter mirrors a stage of growth that every Explorer can relate to—falling into the crevasse of a bad habit, denying its existence, recognizing it yet stumbling again, and finally choosing a new path. This simple narrative is powerful because it reflects our shared struggle to grow, showing that awareness isn’t a one-time act but a journey. Like climbers spotting a crevasse on their trail, we learn to see our “holes”—whether it’s procrastination, fear, or self-doubt—and take responsibility to navigate around them, step by step.
Mindset Shift: Move from “I am defined by my thoughts” to “I am the observer of my thoughts,” creating room for deliberate choice—like a climber pausing to pick a safer route over reacting to a sudden drop or unexpected fork in the path”.
POTENTIAL CHALLENGE:
